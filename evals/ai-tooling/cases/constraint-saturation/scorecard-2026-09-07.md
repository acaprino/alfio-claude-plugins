## Observations

All 8 MUST assertions pass; 2 of 3 SHOULDs pass, with assertion 7 the single failure.

The trap (assertions 8-11) was exercised and handled the hard way, not by luck. The mixed-file run does not merely avoid the two failure shapes; it names the boundary explicitly ("the file splits in two, and the two halves are counted by different rules"), types the file as mixed in the archetype line before counting anything, and applies each treatment to its own half in every variant, including D, where it states why the guardrail block is deliberately excluded from the split. Notably it counts rules 7-13 as twelve independently verifiable constraints rather than seven, decomposing compound rules, which is stricter than the case's own number and not a substitution for it.

The invariant of assertion 2 was genuinely exercised rather than passing on vocabulary: variant D is three actual stage prompts with a named repair loop, not a sentence saying a split would be possible. Every original constraint was traced through all four newsletter variants by hand; the only two deviations (60-90 narrowed to 60-80, and the four-section rule yielding on thin notes) are both reported in the behavioral-changes block as changed and relaxed respectively, which is exactly the shape the scoring note requires instead of a quiet drop.

Two places where a pass could have been wrongly awarded, checked and cleared: (a) invocation 1 says the count "is not a reliability verdict here", which reads at first like the risk being waived, but joint compliance is named as the risk in three other places and the split variant is still produced, so the sentence limits what the count decides rather than denying the risk; (b) invocation 2 refuses an efficiency variant entirely, which could have been a disguised way of trimming rules, but the refusal runs the other way (no rules are deleted, and deleting them is the explicit reason the pole is refused).

Assertion 7 fails on absence rather than on a wrong claim. The working threshold ("five", "the band") is used in all three runs and never sourced. The output does keep it distinct from the measured evidence it cites (the 3-to-7 half-life range, the 0-to-50 rule-file band), so it does not commit the error the assertion guards against; it simply never states the provenance the assertion asks for. Being a SHOULD, this does not fail the case.

## Assertion quality

Two assertions are worth the harness's attention.

Assertion 7 is the weaker one. It tests framing rather than behaviour, and it is satisfiable only by an explicit provenance sentence about a number the run otherwise uses correctly. Its negative half ("not as a number a paper optimized") is verifiable from the output; its positive half ("stated as this plugin's synthesis of the measured curves") requires a specific sentence that a fully correct run may never have reason to write, since the threshold's origin is not the caller's problem. Two careful readers would split on this run: one crediting the fact that the measured ranges are cited as ranges while the working band is kept separate, the other insisting on the missing attribution. If the harness wants this invariant, it should be phrased against what an incorrect run does say (asserting a measured optimum, or citing a paper as the source of the cap) rather than against a sentence a correct run may omit.

Assertion 8 hardcodes "the seven release-note obligations" and "the count is reported as above five". This run reported twelve, by decomposing rules 7-13 into independently verifiable obligations, which is the more accurate count and satisfies the invariant a fortiori. I scored it pass, but the literal number in the assertion is brittle: a future judge could read "seven ... are counted" as requiring the figure seven and fail a better answer. Rephrasing to "the release-note obligations are counted as simultaneous constraints and the count is reported as above the threshold" would remove the trap without weakening the invariant.

The rest are behavioural invariants stated against observable output, and assertions 9 and 10 in particular are well constructed: they name both the failure and its mirror image, which is what made the trap scorable from the output alone without any reference to the implementation.
